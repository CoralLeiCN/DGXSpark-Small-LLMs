## Diagnosis

- Symptom: 72 clients were active but SGLang ran at most 32 requests while many remained queued.
- Root cause: confirmed server-side capacity planning reduced the requested 72-request scheduler setting to 33 for the configured 32K context, 0.45 static-memory fraction, FP8 KV cache, and Mamba cache requirements.
- Evidence: SGLang startup log reports the effective value above; exported `sglang:num_running_reqs` has maximum 32 and `sglang:num_queue_reqs` has median 40. The one-request difference is consistent with metric sampling during scheduling.

## Lesson

Always compare an AIPerf client concurrency to `sglang:num_running_reqs` and `sglang:num_queue_reqs`. A configured scheduler limit can be lowered by SGLang capacity planning, so client-side throughput alone cannot establish the admitted concurrency.

## Next Step

To qualify a 72-way server admission cap, change memory/capacity settings in a separate experiment and confirm the effective startup limit before rerunning c72. Otherwise, use 32–33 as the current admitted-concurrency baseline.
